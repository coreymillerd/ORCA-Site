Maintenance is a cost center. The software should not be one too.

Over 30 years I worked as a technician and a manager in automation, aviation, machine shops and general manufacturing. The hardest part was always the same: keeping track of the equipment.

I tested one CMMS after another. Most were an add-on to some other piece of software, so maintenance was never the main focus, and the price was hard to justify for a department that already costs the company money.

So I built ORCA around the people who actually do the work: operators who report a problem, technicians who fix it, and managers who need to see what is down and what it cost.

What is in the box

Everything a maintenance floor runs on

Works on a phone, tablet or computer. Install it to the home screen and it opens like an app.

Trouble calls

Operators report a problem in seconds, with a machine-down flag so urgent work stands out. Technicians update status, add notes and close it out.

Assets and hours

Every machine in one list. Operators log running hours with a single tap on the asset.

Preventive maintenance

Build PM tasks for each asset, or start from a ready-made template and adjust it.

Parts inventory

Part numbers, quantities on hand, reorder points and shelf locations. Import your whole list from a spreadsheet.

Parts linked to repairs

Record the parts used on each ticket and tie parts to the assets that use them, so history builds itself.

Notes, photos and messages

Every ticket keeps its own repair hours, technician notes, pictures and message thread, with the author on each entry.

Printable repair reports

Open any past ticket and print or save a full PDF report with hours, parts, notes, photos and messages.

Roles that make sense

Admin, manager, technician and operator. Each person sees the screens they need and nothing that gets in the way.

Your data stays yours

Each company is kept separate from every other company. Only what you choose to share is ever visible to anyone else.

What most systems leave out

Share the work, not just the data

Two ORCA features are built around a simple idea: nobody should have to start from scratch or sit on parts someone else needs.

Parts sharing

Find the part before you buy it

Choose which parts you are willing to share. Other ORCA companies can search the shared inventory and send a request, so a rare belt or filter does not mean a week of downtime. You control what is visible, and nothing is shared until you turn it on.

PM template sharing

Start from a template that already works

The ORCA Library holds ready-made PM templates, such as Fanuc machines, that you can copy to your own assets. Use them as they are, or adjust them for your shop.
